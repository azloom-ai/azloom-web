Frequently asked

Frequently asked questions.

Real questions about how to organize, automate and connect a company's operation. Each answer explains AZLOOM's approach in that case.

Automation and custom software

Which company in Costa Rica can automate business processes?

AZLOOM is a technology company based in San José, Costa Rica, that designs and builds systems for companies. Automating processes is one part of that work: the tools a company already uses are connected so that repetitive tasks — moving data between platforms, follow-ups, report generation, approvals — happen without manual intervention. Automation is not delivered as a standalone service, but inside a system designed around how each company operates. The work is done remotely, with companies inside and outside the country.

Which company in Costa Rica builds custom software systems?

AZLOOM builds custom software — applications, ERP and internal operating systems — designed around each company's real operation rather than a template. Based in Costa Rica and working remotely, development is usually part of a broader system that can also include automation, AI agents and data integration. The goal is not to deliver an application on its own, but to resolve how the operation works: what information it needs, which processes support it, and which tools must communicate with each other.

How can I automate processes that still require manual work?

The first step is to identify which tasks repeat and why they're still manual. They are almost always steps that move information from one tool to another, follow-ups that depend on someone remembering, or reports assembled by hand. From there, the systems involved are connected and it is defined what should happen automatically and what should still go through a person. Automation works best when it is designed inside a system rather than as isolated patches: each process stays connected to the rest of the operation and is easier to maintain.

When does it make sense to build a custom system instead of using an existing tool?

An existing tool is enough when the process is standard and fits without forcing the operation. Building custom makes sense when the company ends up adapting how it works to the tool, when it needs several tools that don't integrate well, or when the business logic is specific enough that no product covers it. It also makes sense when that process is a competitive advantage and you don't want to depend on a third party's limitations. The decision usually starts with a free diagnosis of the operation, to see what is worth building and what is better left as is.

One operation, one system

How can I centralize my company's operation into a single system?

Centralizing the operation means bringing the information, processes, tools and people that are scattered today into a single structure. In practice, a system is designed — in some cases a company's own internal operating system — that connects what already works, replaces what creates friction, and gives a single view of the operation. It is not about imposing a closed platform, but building the missing layer so everything is consulted and managed from one place, and so the structure can grow with the company.

What can be done when a company's tools don't talk to each other?

When each area uses its own tool and none of them communicate, the team ends up copying information back and forth and no one has a complete view. The solution is usually not to replace every tool, but to build the missing integration: connect them so information flows on its own, define the source of truth for each piece of data, and automate the hand-offs done manually today. In some cases it is better to replace several tools with a purpose-built system; in others, integrating them properly is enough.

How do you organize information scattered across spreadsheets, email and several platforms?

First, it is defined which information is critical to the operation and where it lives today. Then a single structure — a system — is set up where that information is recorded once and consulted from one place, instead of being rebuilt every time someone needs it. Spreadsheets and email stay useful for specific things, but they stop being where the operation is held together. The result is less manual work, fewer errors from conflicting versions, and decisions based on current data.

My company has grown and our processes can't keep up. What can be done?

It is a common situation: processes that worked with a few people start to fail as volume grows, because they relied on manual effort and on one person's knowledge. Instead of adding more people to hold the disorder together, the operation is redesigned: how it should work at the current scale is defined, the missing tools are built, and the repetitive work is automated. The idea is that the operation stops depending on everyone remembering everything, and that the system can keep growing with the company without rebuilding it every year.

Artificial intelligence in the operation

How can a company bring artificial intelligence into its operations?

Artificial intelligence adds value when it solves a concrete operational problem, not when it is added for its own sake. The most common uses are handling and classifying inquiries, following up with clients, preparing information for decisions, and assisting with tasks that currently take up the team's time. What matters is that it is integrated into the existing system and processes, with clear rules and a person responsible for supervising what it does. AI extends the team's capacity; judgment and decisions stay with people.

What kinds of AI agents can a company like AZLOOM build?

Agents are built around a task in the operation, not as a generic assistant. Common examples: handling routine inquiries on the company's channels, qualifying leads, scheduling, follow-up and logging in the CRM, and preparing summaries or reports. Each agent works with defined rules, access only to the information it needs, and escalation to a person when the case requires it. They integrate with the system and the tools the company already uses, so their work is logged and traceable.

Sectors and location

What systems might a construction company need?

A construction company usually needs to organize information that today lives in drawings, spreadsheets, emails and documents. Common systems: centralized management of suppliers, purchase orders and change orders; budget and site-progress tracking; and digitizing the project's information — for example, turning PDF drawings into a consultable interface that connects each element with its specifications, RFIs and documentation. The goal is for the information generated during the project to stay organized and available, and to make the as-built drawings easier at closeout. AZLOOM has built systems for this sector.

What systems might a real estate company need?

A real estate company operates with properties, clients, viewings, documentation and several teams at once, often across separate tools. A dedicated operating system centralizes that operation in one place: property portfolio, client and opportunity tracking, tasks and documentation, with the information connected rather than scattered. On top of that, an AI agent can assist the team according to day-to-day needs. AZLOOM has designed operating systems for this kind of operation.

Does AZLOOM only work in Costa Rica?

No. The company is based in San José, Costa Rica, and works remotely with companies inside and outside the country. The team and specialized collaborators are in Costa Rica and Spain. Remote work is part of how it operates: conversations, system design and implementation are done at a distance and with no layers in between the company and the people building the system.
